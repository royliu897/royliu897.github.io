GAMMA Lab

A differentiable surrogate for a quantum materials problem.

Research

As a URA in the Group for AI in Materials Modeling and Analysis, my first project was developing a neural network that predicts inelastic neutron scattering data from a material's spin Hamiltonian. The model is differentiable, so it can be used in an inverse problem to estimate those parameters without running a full simulation each time time (aka an INR ↗).

My first-author paper on this work: Observation geometry for uncertainty-aware Hamiltonian inference and experimental design in quantum magnets ↗.

My work

I created a FiLM-modulated SIREN network with 13 inputs: nine Hamiltonian parameters and four energy–momentum coordinates, where the energy-momentum coordinates are conditioned on the given Hamiltonian parameters. This allowed for linear scaling of the amount of training data needed to model 9d Hamiltonians versus 4d Hamiltonians (as done in this work ↗).

A good part of the work was learning how to manipulate the very large dataset (many gigabytes worth of simulated scattering data). I had the privilege of training on Nvidia H100 GPUs through TACC, but still had to use a mix of lazy loading, gradient accumulation, and careful selection of batch sizes to do so in an efficient and effective manner.

Training

The tricky part is that much of the signal is concentrated in narrow Bragg peaks. The model can fit the background fairly well and still miss them, so I had to experiment with weighting losses based on intensity and varying sine frequencies to get the model to work.
