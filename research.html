Research / GAMMA Lab

Learning a faster model of scattering

A differentiable surrogate for a materials inverse problem.

The problem

Direct simulation can make an inverse problem expensive: every candidate set of material parameters needs another prediction. In the GAMMA Lab, I work on a differentiable surrogate that can predict scattering data inside that loop.

The related paper is Observation geometry for uncertainty-aware Hamiltonian inference and experimental design in quantum magnets ↗.

My part

I worked on a modulated SIREN-style network with 13 inputs: nine Hamiltonian parameters and four energy–momentum coordinates.

I also helped bring a large Julia-generated dataset into a Python training workflow on TACC. That involved resolving array-layout and interoperability issues, setting up Slurm jobs, and running GPU training.

What needed care

The useful signal is concentrated in narrow peaks. A model can fit much of the background and still miss the structure we care about. I experimented with weighted losses to give those peaks more influence during training.
